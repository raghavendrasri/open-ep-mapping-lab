Learning hub · Part 1 of 2 — Concepts

Seeing the heart's electricity in 3D

An electrophysiology (EP) mapping system turns the tiny electrical signals picked up by catheters inside the heart into a live, colour-coded 3D map that shows where an abnormal rhythm starts and how it travels.

What it mapsActivation timing, voltage, and anatomy of a heart chamber
What it guidesCatheter navigation and ablation, locating catheters without X-ray Gepstein 1997
Where it is usedProcedures for AF, atrial flutter, SVT, PVCs and ventricular tachycardia

The core idea

From heartbeat to map, in five links

Every mapping system — whatever the brand — is the same chain: sense the heart's local electrical signal, locate the electrode that sensed it in 3D space, and repeat that thousands of times until the points merge into a map. The differences between systems are in how they locate, how densely they sample, and how cleverly they interpret.

01 · SENSE

Electrogram

Electrodes on a catheter inside the chamber record the local activation signal — the electrogram (EGM) — at each spot they touch.

02 · LOCATE

3D position

A magnetic field, impedance field, or dielectric field tells the system where each electrode is, several times per second.

03 · BUILD

Geometry

Sweeping the catheter over the chamber wall traces its shape, building a 3D shell of the atrium or ventricle.

04 · COLOUR

Activation & voltage

Each point is coloured by when it fired (LAT) or how strong its signal was (voltage) — the map a clinician actually reads.

05 · GUIDE

Ablation guidance

The map shows where the source or circuit lies. The clinical team uses it to guide ablation with RF, cryo or pulsed-field energy, and can map again to check the result.

The major mapping platforms, compared

Reading the map

One chamber, many maps

The geometry stays the same; what changes is the value painted onto it. Choosing the right map type is most of the diagnostic skill.

Signal lab

Reading an electrogram

The map is only as good as the signals underneath it. These schematic traces show the four electrogram shapes a mapper learns to recognise on sight. Amplitude and timing — not the picture — are what the system measures.

Hardware

The catheters that do the sensing

High-density mapping became possible when catheters grew from a few electrodes to dozens of small, closely spaced ones. Smaller electrodes with closer spacing record more local signals Anter 2015a.

Multi-spline

PentaRay & OCTARAY (CARTO)

Flexible splines that splay against the chamber wall like a star — five splines carrying 20 electrodes on the PentaRay unverified, eight splines carrying 48 on the OCTARAY Masuda 2024 — giving a wide footprint of points per placement.

Grid paddle

Advisor HD Grid (EnSite)

A flat 4 × 4 paddle of 16 electrodes unverified. Because it samples along two axes at once, the system can compute omnipolar signals Magtibay 2019 — an amplitude that depends much less on the direction the wave crosses the paddle (course module m23).

Mini-basket

IntellaMap Orion (OPAL HDx)

An expandable basket with 64 small electrodes Anter 2015b on 8 splines unverified. Paired with an automated mapping system Sultan 2019, it collects high-density maps — the platform's design goal.

Circular

Lasso-style circular catheters

A loop of electrodes that sits at a pulmonary vein opening and records the vein's potentials — used to check pulmonary vein isolation during AF ablation Anter 2015b.

Ablation

Contact-force ablation catheters

The treatment catheter (e.g. ThermoCool SmartTouch Teijeira-Fernandez 2018; TactiFlex and IntellaNav StablePoint unverified) senses tip contact force in grams, so lesion tags on the map reflect pressure, power and time — not just position. Contact also changes the voltage recorded Teijeira-Fernandez 2018.

Non-contact

AcQMap balloon

A balloon carrying ultrasound transducers and electrodes that hovers in the chamber without touching the wall, reconstructing charge density over the whole chamber at once Pope 2022.

Map-reading checklist

  • Reference stable? — LAT is only meaningful against a steady reference signal
  • Window set right? — in re-entry, maps degrade when the window of interest is shorter than the cycle length Koa-Wing 2015
  • Early meets late? — in re-entry, red should sit next to purple around the circuit
  • Density sufficient? — gaps in points are guesses, not data
  • Voltage direction-checked? — a wave crossing at right angles to a bipole reads falsely low
  • Scar or artefact? — low voltage can also come from poor contact Teijeira-Fernandez 2018 or wave direction
  • EGMs inspected? — the colour is only as good as the signals behind it
